Modeling Portfolio: 10 Photos That Every Model Needs

Molly Curtis December 16, 2023

It's the season of new beginnings, and for models, that often means refreshing your portfolio. When you're just starting out, professional photos may not be necessary — simple snapshots can help you assess your potential. But if an agency shows interest and suggests building your portfolio, you might wonder what they're actually looking for. Here's a breakdown of the shots that matter most.

01
A Natural Beauty Shot
Begin your portfolio with a natural beauty shot that captures your authentic self. A clean, relaxed headshot is the perfect introduction — it allows your features to speak for themselves and sets the tone for the genuine personality that runs through your entire portfolio.
02
A Full-Length Body Shot
Opt for a simple, form-fitting outfit to highlight your figure. This shot offers a comprehensive view of your physical presence and gives potential clients and agencies a clear understanding of your modeling proportions.
03
Editorial Fashion Shot
This is your chance to express your unique style and movement. Choose outfits and poses that showcase your ability to adapt to different fashion concepts, themes, and trends. Demonstrate your range.
04
Professional Headshot
A polished headshot that shows diversity in your expressions and moods. This can also double as a LinkedIn or resume photo — versatility matters here more than anywhere else in the portfolio.
05
A Smiling Shot
Don't underestimate the power of a genuine smile. A lot of models default to serious expressions, but showcasing your warmth and approachability adds a personal touch that makes your portfolio more relatable and inviting.
06
Black and White Shot
Including a black-and-white shot adds sophistication. The absence of color shifts the focus entirely to your form and expression — it's a timeless addition that keeps your portfolio memorable.
07
Swimsuit or Boudoir Shot
Embrace confidence with a shot that highlights your comfort in your own skin. This adds diversity to your portfolio and shows your ability to work in different settings and levels of vulnerability.
08
Casual Shot
A casual shot reflecting your everyday style and personality gives potential clients a glimpse into the person behind the model. It's a refreshing break from posed shots and creates a more authentic connection.
09
Artistic or Conceptual Shot
Show your artistic side with an imaginative, conceptual image. Use this shot to demonstrate your ability to bring creative visions to life — it's a strong signal to agencies that you can handle editorial and high-concept work.
10
A Strong Closing Shot
End on a high note with one of your most impressive photos. This final image should tie everything together and leave a lasting impression — it should feel like a statement, not an afterthought.

Other Shots Worth Considering

  • Close-up and detail shots
  • Candid moments
  • Urban or street style
  • Dramatic lighting setups

Your modeling portfolio is a dynamic work in progress. It should tell your story, represent your brand, and show the direction of your career. Quality always trumps quantity — focus on a few exceptional shots rather than many average ones. As you evolve and gain experience, your portfolio should evolve with you. Shoot with various photographers to explore different styles, and you'll keep improving with each session.
